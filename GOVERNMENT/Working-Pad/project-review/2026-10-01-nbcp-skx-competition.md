Model: J = 0.075 meV, J_z = 0.125 meV, S = 1/2, field along c given as the Zeeman energy h. Grid: J_PD ∈ {0, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.03}, J_Γ ∈ {0, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02} meV, h = 0–0.55 meV in 0.05 steps (240 points).
At each point the classical minimum is found on the 1-, 2-, 3- and 4-site (2 × 2) cells. Each minimum then gets:
- the signed Berg–Lüscher skyrmion number Q per magnetic cell;
- an LSWT stability test on a matched mesh (18 primitive momenta per direction);
- the harmonic energy E_cl + E_zp.

## Results

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Which Q occur in the 4-site cell? | Q = 0, −2 or +2 per four-site cell. A 2 × 2 skyrmion crystal carries one skyrmion per two spins; the triangle-by-triangle Berg–Lüscher sum is an exact integer. |
| Where is the Q ≠ 0 state the classical minimum (by more than 1e-7 meV/spin)? | Within this grid (J_Γ ≤ 0.02 meV), at 4 points, all at h ≤ 0.1 meV and J_PD ≥ 0.02 meV: (J_PD, J_Γ, h) = (0.02, 0.01, 0.05), (0.02, 0.02, 0.05), (0.02, 0.02, 0.1), (0.03, 0.02, 0.1), with Q = −2 for a field along +c. The margin is 0.07–0.8 μeV/spin. Outside this grid the NBCP review thread finds a stable 4-site |Q| = 2 SkX winning already at J_PD = 0 for J_Γ ≳ 0.035–0.05 meV (/mnt/project-files/nbcp-review/phase-diagram/). |
| Sign of Q | At J_PD = J_Γ = 0 the XXZ energy is invariant under the spin-only reflection S^y → −S^y, which reverses Q, so Q = +2 and −2 are degenerate (checked: equal energy to 1e-17 at h = 0.2 meV). With J_PD and J_Γ = 0.01–0.02 meV the reflected state relaxes back to Q = −2 at the points checked, so the couplings select the sign; the field direction alone does not. The sign is not universal: the J_Γ-dominated SkX of the review thread (J_Γ = 0.04–0.05 meV) has Q = +2 for the same field, and its reflection is 21 μeV/spin higher, so the two pockets are distinct textures. |
| Where is it the lowest LSWT-stable candidate? | At the same points and also (0.02, 0.01, 0.1). At all five points it is the only stable candidate: the 1-, 2- and 3-site minima there are unstable in LSWT, which means a modulation with a longer period lowers their energy. |
| At the paper's Fig. 4 coupling J_PD = 0.010 meV | The Q ≠ 0 state never wins. At this coupling it lies at least 0.92 μeV/spin above the best other cell; the closest point is J_Γ = 0.02, h = 0.05 meV. For J_PD ≤ 0.01 the winners are the 3-site (Y/UUD/V), 2-site, uniform and non-topological 4-site states. |
| Larger cells at (0.02, 0.02, 0.1) | 2 × 3 (Q = 0) and 3 × 3 are higher. The 2 × 4 and √12 × √12 minima reproduce the 2 × 2 SkX (Q = −4 and −6, the same energy per spin). A √7 search did not reach a competitive state, so it is not evidence either way. |

## Reading

1. Within the scanned grid (J_PD ≤ 0.03, J_Γ ≤ 0.02 meV) the four-site SkX is a classical and harmonic candidate only for J_PD ≳ 0.02 meV with J_Γ ≳ 0.01 meV at low field, and is not selected at the paper's Fig. 4 coupling J_PD = 0.010 meV with J_Γ ≤ 0.02 meV. Larger J_Γ (beyond this grid) makes it win even at J_PD = 0 (NBCP review thread).
2. At the points where it wins, every smaller-cell competitor is unstable. Incommensurate or larger-cell states not scanned here could still be lower, so these points are SkX candidates, not an established phase.
3. The signed charge settles the open item of chapter 5. The texture is a |Q| = 2 per 2 × 2 cell skyrmion crystal. Its sign is degenerate at J_PD = J_Γ = 0 and selected by the bond-dependent couplings otherwise (see the table). The absolute-value solid angle of the original draft cannot give this.
4. This scan uses the note's Hamiltonian at harmonic order only. It does not tell which parameter set the paper used for its SkX region; that needs the paper's figure parameters.
