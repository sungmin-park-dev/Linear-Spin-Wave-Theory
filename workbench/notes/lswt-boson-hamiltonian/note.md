# Holstein–Primakoff Expansion

The Holstein–Primakoff representation expresses fluctuations about a local spin direction in terms of boson operators. The local axes are defined in Classical Order and Local Frame in research note *LSWT classical order*.

## Leading Spin Operators

At the order used in linear spin-wave theory, the transverse spin operators are

$$
\hat{\widetilde S}_I^+
=\sqrt{2S_I}\,\hat a_I+O(S_I^{-1/2}),
\qquad
\hat{\widetilde S}_I^-
=\sqrt{2S_I}\,\hat a_I^\dagger+O(S_I^{-1/2}),
$$

and the longitudinal component is

$$
\hat{\widetilde S}_I^0=S_I-\hat n_I,
\qquad
\hat n_I=\hat a_I^\dagger\hat a_I.
$$

Here, $S_I$ is the spin length, and $\hat n_I$ counts the local Holstein–Primakoff bosons. The superscripts $\pm$ denote the local raising and lowering components, while $0$ denotes the longitudinal component. The boson occupation measures the reduction of that longitudinal spin component from $S_I$.

## Fluctuations and Classical Stationarity

The spin fluctuation separates into transverse and longitudinal parts relative to the local direction $\mathbf e_I^0$:

$$
\delta\hat{\mathbf S}_I
=\delta\hat{\mathbf S}_I^\perp
 +\delta\hat{\mathbf S}_I^\parallel,
\qquad
\delta\hat{\mathbf S}_I^\parallel=-\hat n_I\mathbf e_I^0.
$$

When the reference configuration is an extremum of the classical energy under fixed-spin-length constraints, the linear boson terms vanish. With a Lagrange multiplier $\lambda_I$ for the constraint at each site,

$$
\frac{\partial E_{\mathrm{cl}}}{\partial\mathbf S_I}
=\lambda_I\mathbf S_I,
\qquad
\frac{\partial E_{\mathrm{cl}}}{\partial\mathbf S_I}
\cdot\delta\hat{\mathbf S}_I^\perp=0.
$$

The energy gradient is parallel to the classical spin, whereas the leading transverse fluctuation is perpendicular to it. Their vanishing contraction removes the linear contribution. The quadratic terms generated by the expansion are collected in Real-Space Boson Hamiltonian (Section *Real-Space Boson Hamiltonian*).

# Real-Space Boson Hamiltonian

The quadratic boson Hamiltonian collects the terms of second order in the Holstein–Primakoff operators. The expansion below covers inter-site exchange, the Zeeman term and, in the last subsection, the single-ion term with matrix $\mathsf D_I$. The local frame and its circular basis vectors $\mathbf e_I^\pm$, $\mathbf e_I^0=\mathbf n_I$ are those of Local Circular Basis in research note *LSWT classical order*.

## Spin Operator in the Local Circular Basis

The transverse local components combine into the ladder operators $\hat{\widetilde S}_I^\pm=\hat{\widetilde S}_I^x\pm\mathrm i\hat{\widetilde S}_I^y$, and the laboratory spin operator reads

$$
\hat{\mathbf S}_I
=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\left(\hat{\widetilde S}_I^+\mathbf e_I^-+\hat{\widetilde S}_I^-\mathbf e_I^+\right)
+\hat{\widetilde S}_I^0\,\mathbf e_I^0 .
$$

The bilinear products $\mathbf e_I^\pm\cdot\mathbf e_I^\mp=1$ and $\mathbf e_I^\pm\cdot\mathbf e_I^\pm=0$ give $\mathbf e_I^+\cdot\hat{\mathbf S}_I=\hat{\widetilde S}_I^+/\sqrt2$, so the raising operator is the component along $\mathbf e_I^+$ and its coefficient vector in the expansion is the dual vector $\mathbf e_I^-$. Inserting the leading forms of Holstein–Primakoff Expansion (Section *Holstein–Primakoff Expansion*) gives

$$
\hat{\mathbf S}_I
=\left(S_I-\hat n_I\right)\mathbf e_I^0
+\sqrt{S_I}\left(\mathbf e_I^-\hat a_I+\mathbf e_I^+\hat a_I^\dagger\right)
+O(S_I^{-1/2}).
$$

The omitted terms of the transverse part contain three boson operators, so they contribute to the Hamiltonian only at cubic and higher order. The longitudinal part $S_I-\hat n_I$ is exact. A boson annihilation operator therefore always comes with $\mathbf e_I^-$ and a creation operator with $\mathbf e_I^+$.

## Circular Components of the Exchange Matrix

For a link $\ell=(I,J)$ with real laboratory exchange matrix $\mathsf J_\ell$, we define the circular components

$$
\widetilde J_\ell^{pq}
=\left(\mathbf e_I^p\right)^{\mathsf T}\mathsf J_\ell\,\mathbf e_J^q,
\qquad p,q\in\{+,-,0\},
$$

where the first label refers to the first endpoint $I$ and the second label to $J$. The contraction is bilinear; no complex conjugate is taken. Equivalently, $\widetilde J_\ell^{pq}$ are the components of the local-frame matrix $\widetilde{\mathsf J}_\ell=\mathsf R_I^{\mathsf T}\mathsf J_\ell\mathsf R_J$ of Rotation to the Local Frame in research note *LSWT classical order* in the basis $\{(\mathbf e_x\pm\mathrm i\mathbf e_y)/\sqrt2,\mathbf e_z\}$. Because $\mathsf J_\ell$ is real and $\mathbf e_I^-=(\mathbf e_I^+)^*$,

$$
\widetilde J_\ell^{-+}=\big(\widetilde J_\ell^{+-}\big)^*,
\qquad
\widetilde J_\ell^{--}=\big(\widetilde J_\ell^{++}\big)^*,
\qquad
\widetilde J_\ell^{00}\in\mathbb R .
$$

The reverse orientation $\bar\ell=(J,I)$ has $\mathsf J_{\bar\ell}=\mathsf J_\ell^{\mathsf T}$, so its components are the transposed ones, $\widetilde J_{\bar\ell}^{qp}=\widetilde J_\ell^{pq}$. This relation completes the reverse-bond rule of Links and Bond Orientation in research note *LSWT spin Hamiltonian* for the circular basis. The same definition applies to the single-ion matrix with both labels on the same site, $\widetilde D_I^{pq}=(\mathbf e_I^p)^{\mathsf T}\mathsf D_I\mathbf e_I^q$; the symmetry of $\mathsf D_I$ gives in addition $\widetilde D_I^{pq}=\widetilde D_I^{qp}$.

## Quadratic Exchange and Field Terms

For a link $\ell=(I,J)$, we define the spin-weighted coefficients

$$
t_\ell^{pq}=\sqrt{S_I S_J}\,\widetilde J_\ell^{pq},
\qquad p,q\in\{+,-\}.
$$

Inserting the expansion of the spin operator into $\hat{\mathbf S}_I^{\mathsf T}\mathsf J_\ell\hat{\mathbf S}_J$ and keeping the terms of second order in the bosons gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hat H_2
=&\sum_{\ell=(I,J)\in\mathcal L}\Big[
t_\ell^{--}\hat a_I\hat a_J
+t_\ell^{-+}\hat a_I\hat a_J^\dagger
+t_\ell^{+-}\hat a_I^\dagger\hat a_J
+t_\ell^{++}\hat a_I^\dagger\hat a_J^\dagger
-\widetilde J_\ell^{00}\left(S_I\hat n_J+S_J\hat n_I\right)
\Big]\\
&+\sum_I\widetilde h_I^0\,\hat n_I .
\end{aligned}
$$

The labels on $t_\ell^{pq}$ follow the basis vectors: a label $-$ accompanies an annihilation operator and a label $+$ a creation operator. The first four terms are the normal and anomalous boson products of the link; the longitudinal term comes from the product $(S_I-\hat n_I)(S_J-\hat n_J)$, whose part $\hat n_I\hat n_J$ is quartic and is dropped. The Zeeman term $-\mathbf h_I\cdot\hat{\mathbf S}_I$ of Bilinear Spin Hamiltonian in research note *LSWT spin Hamiltonian* contributes through its longitudinal part, with

$$
\widetilde h_I^0=\mathbf h_I\cdot\mathbf n_I ,
$$

the projection of the Zeeman-energy vector on the classical spin, which is the third component of $\widetilde{\mathbf h}_I=\mathsf R_I^{\mathsf T}\mathbf h_I$. A field along the spin raises the energy of a spin deviation, as expected for a spin that the field favors.

The reality relations above make the link term Hermitian: $t_\ell^{-+}\hat a_I\hat a_J^\dagger$ is the conjugate of $t_\ell^{+-}\hat a_I^\dagger\hat a_J$, and $t_\ell^{--}\hat a_I\hat a_J$ that of $t_\ell^{++}\hat a_I^\dagger\hat a_J^\dagger$. Operators at the distinct sites $I\ne J$ commute, so the order of the two factors in each product is immaterial and the inter-site terms produce no additive constant. The terms linear in the bosons, of order $S^{3/2}$, vanish at a stationary reference configuration, as shown in Fluctuations and Classical Stationarity (Section *Holstein–Primakoff Expansion*); they are not included in $\hat H_2$.

## Local Number-Operator Coefficient

Each site receives longitudinal exchange contributions from all incident links. Let $\partial I$ be the set of links incident on $I$, and let $I'_\ell$ be the opposite endpoint of link $\ell$. For the exchange and field terms displayed above, the coefficient of $\hat n_I$ is

$$
\mu_I
=\widetilde h_I^0
-\sum_{\ell\in\partial I}S_{I'_\ell}\widetilde J_\ell^{00}.
$$

This coefficient is the projection on $\mathbf n_I$ of the local field $\mathbf h_I-\sum_{\ell\in\partial I}S_{I'_\ell}\mathsf J_{II'_\ell}\mathbf n_{I'_\ell}$, the Zeeman-energy vector plus the exchange field of the neighboring classical spins, with $\mathsf J_{II'}$ oriented as in Link Counting in research note *LSWT spin Hamiltonian*. Without single-ion anisotropy, a stationary configuration has every spin parallel or antiparallel to its local field, and $\mu_I$ equals the magnitude of that field when the spin is parallel to it and minus the magnitude when antiparallel. The single-ion term below adds to $\mu_I$.

## Single-Ion Term

The single-ion term $\hat{\mathbf S}_I^{\mathsf T}\mathsf D_I\hat{\mathbf S}_I$ contains two spin operators of the same site, and their product is not determined by the classical vector alone. We use the rule that reproduces the spin-coherent expectation value of the classical energy in LSWT Overview in research note *LSWT spin Hamiltonian*: the term enters the quadratic Hamiltonian as a self-coupling of site $I$, expanded like a link whose two endpoints coincide, with $\mathsf D_I$ replaced by

$$
\Big(1-\frac{1}{2S_I}\Big)\mathsf D_I,
$$

and without the constant produced by normal-ordering the transverse operators of the same site, $\hat a_I\hat a_I^\dagger=\hat a_I^\dagger\hat a_I+1$. With $\kappa_I=1-1/(2S_I)$ and the symmetry $\widetilde D_I^{+-}=\widetilde D_I^{-+}$, the quadratic contribution is

$$
\hat H_{2,\mathrm{ion}}
=\sum_I\kappa_I S_I\Big[
\widetilde D_I^{--}\hat a_I\hat a_I
+\widetilde D_I^{++}\hat a_I^\dagger\hat a_I^\dagger
+2\big(\widetilde D_I^{+-}-\widetilde D_I^{00}\big)\hat n_I
\Big].
$$

The corresponding classical energy is $S_I(S_I-\tfrac12)\,\mathbf n_I^{\mathsf T}\mathsf D_I\mathbf n_I+\tfrac{S_I}{2}\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf D_I$; at $S_I=\tfrac12$ it is the constant $\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf D_I/4$ and the term drops out of the quadratic Hamiltonian, as the spin-$\tfrac12$ identity of Bilinear Spin Hamiltonian in research note *LSWT spin Hamiltonian* requires.

For an easy-axis term $D(\hat S_I^z)^2$ with $D<0$ and the spin along $z$, only $\widetilde D_I^{00}=D$ is nonzero, and the single-ion contribution is $-2S_I(1-\tfrac1{2S_I})D\,\hat n_I=(2S_I-1)|D|\,\hat n_I$. The resulting single-ion gap $(2S_I-1)|D|$ equals the exact spacing between the levels $m=S_I$ and $m=S_I-1$, whereas the factor $2S_I$ of a direct expansion with $\mathsf D_I$ overestimates it. The rule is justified by these two agreements, with the coherent-state classical energy and with the exact single-ion gap, rather than derived from the Holstein–Primakoff expansion of the operator product.

# Momentum-Space BdG Hamiltonian

For a commensurate ordered state, the momentum-space LSWT problem is defined in the magnetic Brillouin zone, denoted by $\mathrm{MBZ}$. The derivation uses $N_{\mathrm{uc}}$ magnetic unit cells with periodic boundary conditions and $N_{\mathrm{sub}}$ magnetic sublattices per cell. Magnetic translation symmetry makes the local frame depend only on the sublattice, $\mathsf R_{i\mu}=\mathsf R_\mu$, so the circular components $\widetilde J_\ell^{pq}$, the coefficients $t_\ell^{pq}$ and the coefficients $\mu_I=\mu_\mu$ are the same for all translates of a link or a site.

## Fourier Representation

The full-position Fourier convention used in the expressions below is

$$
\hat a_{i\mu}
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{uc}}}}
\sum_{\mathbf k\in\mathrm{MBZ}}
\exp(\mathrm i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r_{i\mu})\,
\hat a_{\mathbf k\mu}.
$$

Here, $\mathbf r_{i\mu}=\mathbf R_i+\boldsymbol\delta_\mu$ is the full position of the spin site, and the $N_{\mathrm{uc}}$ momenta of the MBZ are those allowed by the periodic boundary conditions. The transformation is unitary, so the $\hat a_{\mathbf k\mu}$ obey canonical commutation relations. For a link $\ell=((i,\mu),(j,\nu))$ with displacement $\boldsymbol\Delta_\ell=\mathbf r_{j\nu}-\mathbf r_{i\mu}$, summing a boson product over the $N_{\mathrm{uc}}$ translates of the link gives

$$
\sum_{\text{translates}}\hat a_I^\dagger\hat a_J
=\sum_{\mathbf k}\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\mathbf k\cdot\boldsymbol\Delta_\ell}\,\hat a_{\mathbf k\mu}^\dagger\hat a_{\mathbf k\nu},
\qquad
\sum_{\text{translates}}\hat a_I^\dagger\hat a_J^\dagger
=\sum_{\mathbf k}\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\mathbf k\cdot\boldsymbol\Delta_\ell}\,\hat a_{\mathbf k\mu}^\dagger\hat a_{-\mathbf k\nu}^\dagger,
$$

together with the Hermitian conjugates of these relations and $\sum_i\hat n_{i\mu}=\sum_{\mathbf k}\hat a_{\mathbf k\mu}^\dagger\hat a_{\mathbf k\mu}$. Only the displacement enters, because the phases of the two endpoints combine as $\exp[\mathrm i\mathbf k\cdot(\mathbf r_J-\mathbf r_I)]$ once the lattice sum enforces momentum conservation. The adoption of this convention is listed under Conventions Not Yet Fixed in Notation and Conventions in research note *LSWT spin Hamiltonian*.

With full positions, the matrices below are not periodic under a reciprocal-lattice vector $\mathbf G$ of the magnetic lattice; each entry $(\mu,\nu)$ acquires the factor $\exp[\mathrm i\mathbf G\cdot(\boldsymbol\delta_\nu-\boldsymbol\delta_\mu)]$, so that $\mathsf H_{\mathbf k+\mathbf G}=\Lambda_{\mathbf G}\mathsf H_{\mathbf k}\Lambda_{\mathbf G}^\dagger$ with the diagonal unitary $\Lambda_{\mathbf G}=\mathsf I_2\otimes\operatorname{diag}_\mu\exp(-\mathrm i\mathbf G\cdot\boldsymbol\delta_\mu)$, the same phase in both Nambu blocks. This is the statement $\hat a_{\mathbf k+\mathbf G,\mu}=\exp(-\mathrm i\mathbf G\cdot\boldsymbol\delta_\mu)\hat a_{\mathbf k\mu}$ of the Fourier convention. The magnon energies are periodic, while the eigenvectors acquire the sublattice phases.

## Nambu Ordering and BdG Blocks

We order the Nambu spinor so that its Hermitian conjugate is

$$
\hat\Psi_{\mathbf k}^\dagger
=\left(
\hat a_{\mathbf k1}^\dagger,\ldots,
\hat a_{\mathbf k N_{\mathrm{sub}}}^\dagger,
\hat a_{-\mathbf k1},\ldots,
\hat a_{-\mathbf k N_{\mathrm{sub}}}
\right).
$$

The quadratic Hamiltonian is written in bosonic Bogoliubov–de Gennes (BdG) form as

$$
\hat H_2
=\frac12\sum_{\mathbf k\in\mathrm{MBZ}}
\left(
\hat\Psi_{\mathbf k}^\dagger\mathsf H_{\mathbf k}\hat\Psi_{\mathbf k}
-\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf A_{\mathbf k}
\right),
$$

with block matrix

$$
\mathsf H_{\mathbf k}
=\begin{pmatrix}
\mathsf A_{\mathbf k} & \mathsf B_{\mathbf k}\\
\mathsf B_{\mathbf k}^\dagger & \mathsf A_{-\mathbf k}^*
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The normal block $\mathsf A_{\mathbf k}$ and anomalous block $\mathsf B_{\mathbf k}$ satisfy

$$
\mathsf A_{\mathbf k}^\dagger=\mathsf A_{\mathbf k},
\qquad
\mathsf B_{-\mathbf k}=\mathsf B_{\mathbf k}^{\mathsf T}.
$$

## Normal and Anomalous Blocks

Applying the Fourier relations to the real-space Hamiltonian gives the entries of the two blocks. For an ordered pair of sublattices $(\mu,\nu)$, let $\mathcal L_{\mu\nu}$ be the links of one magnetic unit cell, one per translation class, whose first endpoint lies on sublattice $\mu$ and second endpoint on sublattice $\nu$. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\mathsf A_{\mathbf k})_{\mu\nu}
&=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal L_{\mu\nu}}t_\ell^{+-}\,\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\mathbf k\cdot\boldsymbol\Delta_\ell}
+\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal L_{\nu\mu}}t_\ell^{-+}\,\mathrm e^{-\mathrm i\mathbf k\cdot\boldsymbol\Delta_\ell}
+\delta_{\mu\nu}\big(\mu_\mu+2\kappa_\mu S_\mu\big(\widetilde D_\mu^{+-}-\widetilde D_\mu^{00}\big)\big),\\
(\mathsf B_{\mathbf k})_{\mu\nu}
&=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal L_{\mu\nu}}t_\ell^{++}\,\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\mathbf k\cdot\boldsymbol\Delta_\ell}
+\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal L_{\nu\mu}}t_\ell^{++}\,\mathrm e^{-\mathrm i\mathbf k\cdot\boldsymbol\Delta_\ell}
+2\delta_{\mu\nu}\,\kappa_\mu S_\mu\widetilde D_\mu^{++}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The second sum in each block is the contribution of a link read in the reverse direction: for a link in $\mathcal L_{\nu\mu}$ the operator $\hat a_{\mathbf k\mu}^\dagger$ belongs to its second endpoint. When $\mu=\nu$, a same-sublattice link contributes to both sums, so its normal term becomes $2\operatorname{Re}[t_\ell^{+-}\exp(\mathrm i\mathbf k\cdot\boldsymbol\Delta_\ell)]$ and its anomalous term $2t_\ell^{++}\cos(\mathbf k\cdot\boldsymbol\Delta_\ell)$. The anomalous entries are written so that each pair product appears symmetrically in $\tfrac12(\hat a_{\mathbf k\mu}^\dagger(\mathsf B_{\mathbf k})_{\mu\nu}\hat a_{-\mathbf k\nu}^\dagger+\text{h.c.})$; the relation $\hat a_{\mathbf k\mu}^\dagger\hat a_{-\mathbf k\nu}^\dagger=\hat a_{-\mathbf k\nu}^\dagger\hat a_{\mathbf k\mu}^\dagger$ and the relabeling $\mathbf k\to-\mathbf k$ split each link product into the two entries $(\mu,\nu)$ and $(\nu,\mu)$. The reality relation $t_\ell^{-+}=(t_\ell^{+-})^*$ gives $\mathsf A_{\mathbf k}^\dagger=\mathsf A_{\mathbf k}$, and the explicit symmetry of the two anomalous sums gives $\mathsf B_{-\mathbf k}=\mathsf B_{\mathbf k}^{\mathsf T}$.

## Trace Subtraction

With these blocks, the Nambu form reproduces the real-space quadratic Hamiltonian exactly, including its additive constant. Expanding the Nambu product,

$$
\frac12\sum_{\mathbf k}\hat\Psi_{\mathbf k}^\dagger\mathsf H_{\mathbf k}\hat\Psi_{\mathbf k}
=\sum_{\mathbf k}\Big[\hat{\mathbf a}_{\mathbf k}^\dagger\mathsf A_{\mathbf k}\hat{\mathbf a}_{\mathbf k}
+\tfrac12\big(\hat{\mathbf a}_{\mathbf k}^\dagger\mathsf B_{\mathbf k}(\hat{\mathbf a}_{-\mathbf k}^\dagger)^{\mathsf T}+\text{h.c.}\big)\Big]
+\frac12\sum_{\mathbf k}\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf A_{\mathbf k},
$$

where the constant comes from commuting the hole components, $\hat a_{-\mathbf k\mu}\hat a_{-\mathbf k\nu}^\dagger=\hat a_{-\mathbf k\nu}^\dagger\hat a_{-\mathbf k\mu}+\delta_{\mu\nu}$, and from relabeling $-\mathbf k\to\mathbf k$ in a momentum set closed under inversion. The first bracket is the normal-ordered real-space $\hat H_2$. That Hamiltonian has no constant of its own: the inter-site products commute, the longitudinal part $S_I-\hat n_I$ is exact, and the single-ion rule omits the same-site normal-ordering constant. The subtraction of $\frac12\sum_{\mathbf k}\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf A_{\mathbf k}$ therefore removes exactly the constant created by the symmetric Nambu form, and $E_{\mathrm{cl}}+\hat H_2$ is the spin Hamiltonian to quadratic order, with $E_{\mathrm{cl}}$ the classical energy including the coherent-state single-ion value $S_I(S_I-\tfrac12)\mathbf n_I^{\mathsf T}\mathsf D_I\mathbf n_I+\tfrac{S_I}{2}\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf D_I$. The zero-point correction of Diagonal Magnon Hamiltonian and Zero-Point Correction in research note *LSWT paraunitary diagonalization* inherits this normalization.

## Example: Square-Lattice Néel Antiferromagnet

The nearest-neighbor Heisenberg antiferromagnet $\mathsf J_\ell=J\mathsf I_3$, $J>0$, on the square lattice with unit lattice constant, zero field and spin $S$ on every site illustrates the blocks. The Néel configuration has two magnetic sublattices, $\mathbf n_A=\mathbf e_z$ and $\mathbf n_B=-\mathbf e_z$, and each $A$ site has four links to $B$ sites with displacements $\boldsymbol\Delta\in\{\pm\mathbf e_x,\pm\mathbf e_y\}$. The rotation $\mathsf R(\pi,0)=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,-1)$ gives $\mathbf e_B^\pm=-\mathbf e_A^\mp$ with $\mathbf e_A^\pm=(\mathbf e_x\pm\mathrm i\mathbf e_y)/\sqrt2$. With $\mathbf e_A^\pm\cdot\mathbf e_A^\pm=0$ and $\mathbf e_A^+\cdot\mathbf e_A^-=1$, every link has

$$
\widetilde J^{+-}=0,
\qquad
\widetilde J^{++}=-J,
\qquad
\widetilde J^{00}=-J .
$$

The normal block is diagonal, $\mathsf A_{\mathbf k}=4JS\,\mathsf I_2$, and the anomalous block is off-diagonal,

$$
\mathsf B_{\mathbf k}=-4JS\gamma_{\mathbf k}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\gamma_{\mathbf k}=\tfrac12(\cos k_x+\cos k_y).
$$

Paraunitary diagonalization gives the doubly degenerate band $\varepsilon_{\mathbf k}=4JS\sqrt{1-\gamma_{\mathbf k}^2}$, and the trace subtraction gives the zero-point correction per site

$$
\frac{\Delta E_{\mathrm{zp}}}{N_{\mathrm{site}}}
=\frac{1}{2N_{\mathrm{uc}}}\sum_{\mathbf k\in\mathrm{MBZ}}\big(\varepsilon_{\mathbf k}-4JS\big)
=-2JS\,C,
\qquad
C=1-\Big\langle\sqrt{1-\gamma_{\mathbf k}^2}\Big\rangle\simeq0.157947,
$$

where $\langle\cdot\rangle$ is the average over the Brillouin zone. Together with $E_{\mathrm{cl}}/N_{\mathrm{site}}=-2JS^2$, this is the known LSWT energy $-2JS^2-0.315895\,JS$ of the square-lattice antiferromagnet (Zheng and Hamer, Sec. IV). A direct numerical evaluation of the general block formulas on a $240\times240$ mesh reproduces the coefficient $-0.315895$ to six digits.
